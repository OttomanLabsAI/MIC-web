The route

What "easy to moderate" actually means

Box Hill rises straight out of the Mole valley, so the walk starts high, drops to the river and climbs back. Here is the profile, so you can decide for yourself rather than take our word for it.

The descent is gentle and mostly on made paths. The return climb is the only sustained effort — steady, not steep, with plenty of stops. Most people walking at a family pace finish in two and a half to three hours.

Before you come

What to bring

Box Hill is chalk. Chalk in September is fine when dry and genuinely slippery when wet, so footwear is the one thing worth getting right.

  • 01Proper footwearWalking boots or trainers with real grip. Not smooth soles, not sandals.
  • 02A waterproofIt is the North Downs in September. Bring it even if the forecast is clear.
  • 03Water and a snackAt least a litre each. There is a café at the summit but nothing on the route.
  • 04LayersIt is noticeably colder and windier at the viewpoint than in the car park.
  • 05Your fundraising linkSet it up before the day so you can share photos as you walk.

Getting there

Make your own way
— here is how

Meeting point Must be confirmed

There are five car parks at Box Hill

Main, East, Old Fort, Whitehill and Stepping Stones — and they are not walking distance from each other. Saying "the car park" will split the group before we start.

Publish one car park by name, a postcode, a what3words and a map pin, plus a phone number for the day. This is the single biggest cause of no-shows on group walks.

By car

Box Hill Main car park
Zig Zag Road, Box Hill
Surrey KT20 7LB

Parking is pay and display and National Trust members park free. Expect roughly £6 for four hours or £9 all day — check current rates before you travel. Blue badge holders park free.

The Zig Zag Road is narrow with tight hairpins and is not suitable for large vehicles.

By train

Box Hill & Westhumble is the nearest station, roughly a mile and a half from the summit — a proper uphill walk in itself, so allow extra time or arrange a lift from the station.

By bus

The 21 runs Monday to Saturday between Crawley, Dorking, Leatherhead and Epsom and stops at the summit by the East car park.

Accessibility

There are accessible paths to the viewpoint and along the North Downs Way, accessible parking at the Old Fort car park, and an all-terrain push wheelchair the National Trust lends free of charge if booked in advance. Tell us what you need and we will plan the route around it.

Before you register

Questions people
actually ask

Do I have to pay the £50 as well as the £10?

No. The £10 is a registration fee you pay when you book. The £50 is a fundraising target — money you ask other people to donate. If you are worried about hitting it, come anyway and raise what you can. Confirm this is the intended policy

Which car park exactly?

Box Hill has five. The meeting point will be confirmed here and emailed to everyone who registers, with a map pin and a number to call on the day. Confirm and publish

How fit do I need to be?

If you can walk for an hour without stopping, you will be fine. There is one sustained climb on the way back and we go at the pace of the slowest person, with breaks. Nobody gets left behind.

Can I bring my children?

Yes — families are the point. Confident walkers from around six manage it well, and smaller ones are fine in a carrier. The paths are not suitable for pushchairs. Children must stay with a responsible adult throughout. Confirm age guidance

Is there somewhere to pray?

Zuhr falls during the walk in September, so we stop together as a group at a suitable point on the route. Bring a mat or something to lay down. There is no indoor prayer facility on the hill. Confirm timing

What happens if the weather is bad?

We walk in light rain — the chalk gets slippery but the made paths stay walkable. In heavy rain or high wind we shorten the route rather than cancel. Any change goes out on the WhatsApp group and by email by 8am on the day. Confirm policy

Can I bring my dog?

Dogs are welcome on the hill and must be on a lead near the grazing livestock. Let us know when you register so we can seat groups sensibly at the start. Confirm MIC policy

Is transport provided?

No — please make your own way to and from Box Hill. If you have a spare seat or need one, the WhatsApp group is the place to arrange lift shares.

I have never been to an MIC event. Should I come alone?

Most people do. A walk is the easiest possible introduction — there is no seating plan to worry about, you fall into conversation as you go, and three hours side by side does more than an evening across a table.
